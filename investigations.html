1. Tehama / Shasta County Roster Investigation

A public-payroll roster of county government — who works where, and what the public record says about them.

  • 2,724 people in the roster: Tehama County 883, Shasta County 1,839, plus 2 supplementary — built from Transparent California 2025 payroll, a public source.
  • 23 cross-county surname matches — the same surname appearing in both counties. A shared surname is a search lead only, never evidence of a relationship.
  • 37-entry accountability list — escaped prosecution, Brady/Giglio listings and dodges, resignations, transfers, firings, and scandals by name.
  • Criteria table A1–A60: 46 findings read "cannot determine," 7 non-compliance, 4 established, 2 compliant in form, 1 framework. All 60 ongoing, none resolved. The table maps uncertainty — that is what an audit needs.
Method: public payroll, public names, no records requests. Sources that already name people — agenda packets, budgets, grand jury reports, annual reports, press releases, FPPC Form 700, CalPERS and Transparent California, State Bar records, judicial orders — plus photographed lobby rosters.

2. Shasta County Flock ALPR Investigation

Shasta County Sheriff's Office Flock license-plate cameras — installed first, public comment asked for after.

  • SCSO asked the community to email opinions on Flock cameras on November 10, 2026 — after the cameras were already in place.
  • The community response was overwhelmingly opposed: cameras installed without asking, the DA's office with 1,000+ incidents unaddressed (a commenter's claim), and Fourth Amendment concerns raised by residents.
  • Flock reads license plates and vehicle attributes. Flock states it does no facial biometric analysis, issues no traffic citations, and does not track individuals. SCSO states it controls sharing and deletes data after 30 days.
Unverified allegation: a commenter alleged Zogg fire fund money was used for Flock cameras instead of going to fire victims. That is a commenter's allegation, not a fact. It is flagged for follow-up — see investigation 3 below.

3. Zogg Fire Money — Where Did It Go?

The Zogg Fire was a 2020 wildfire in Shasta County. It burned about 56,000 acres, destroyed 204 structures, and killed 4 people. Relief money was raised for the victims. The question is simple: where did it go?

  • How much was raised and collected for Zogg fire victims?
  • Who held the money, and where was it disbursed?
  • Did it reach the victims?
Connection to investigation 2: a commenter alleged Zogg fire fund money was spent on Flock cameras instead of fire victims. That allegation is unverified — a commenter's allegation, not a fact. This investigation traces the money itself.

Vote: should the Zogg fire money spending be made public?

4. Stephanie Bridgett and the People's Satisfaction Rating

Stephanie Bridgett is the Shasta County District Attorney. This investigation is the people's satisfaction rating of the District Attorney's office — citizens rate their satisfaction, and the rating is tracked in the open.

  • The rating is voted by the citizens, not set by the office itself.
  • Community members have raised concerns in public comment about unaddressed cases; those concerns are community comment, not verified findings.
  • The investigation follows the DA's office performance as the people experience it, and reports the rating as it stands.
