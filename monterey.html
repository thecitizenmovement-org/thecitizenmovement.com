● Monterey County, California

Marked November 10, 2026. Investigative alert, not a finding and not a court conclusion.

Monterey County’s child-welfare department has a documented pattern of removing children without a warrant. A grandmother’s account and the federal court record tell the same story.

Files

  • Federal civil rights case — 2018 removal of two children from school without a judge’s order; summary judgment denied to the county in 2021; settled March 2022 for $1.2 million.
  • Puga et al v. Monterey County Department of Social & Employment Services, No. 5:25-cv-04655-BLF (N.D. Cal.) — filed June 3, 2025; motions to dismiss granted in part and denied in part, December 12, 2025. Still active.
  • Kathrine Thomas — federal complaint filed December 2025, raising equitable tolling, a Monell claim, and a civil-rights claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. Case number outstanding.
  • Kathrine Thomas — email to the movement, November 9, 2026; public interview, November 8, 2026.

Facts — the court record

  • May 4, 2018 — the county removed two children from school without a warrant or a judge’s order. The juvenile case was dismissed after a contested trial.
  • March 26, 2021 — a federal judge denied the county summary judgment, ruling the warrant requirement was clearly established long before the removal.
  • A county supervisor testified that her workers removed children 20 to 30 times and never once obtained a warrant. The federal court found a jury could call the county’s training deliberate indifference.
  • March 2022 — the court approved a $1.2 million settlement: $600,000 to the family’s lawyers, $110,000 in costs, $245,000 to the mother, $122,500 to each child. No admission of wrongdoing.
  • November 28, 2023 — a second warrantless-removal attempt; law enforcement refused to act without a warrant. The children were placed with a relative under a safety plan, then removed to foster care. The case was later dismissed and the children returned; a state appellate court reversed in January 2025.

Facts — Kathrine Thomas’s account

  • 2012 — her infant grandson was removed from the family’s care at about one month old. She was denied placement, even though the parents had attempted to safely surrender him into her care at the hospital, and a hospital social worker had noted the surrender.
  • At a hospital team decision meeting, she raised the safe-surrender question and was told to be quiet or she would be removed from the meeting.
  • 2012–2015 — she appealed and lost. The child was adopted out under a closed adoption.
  • October 2025 — she found a settlement document from a federal lawsuit out of Monterey County, obtained through a California Public Records Act request after a federal records request was denied.
  • December 2025 — she filed a federal complaint, arguing that judicial deception should affect the statute-of-limitations analysis.
